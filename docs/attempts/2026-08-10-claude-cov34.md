## What this rung actually asks

cov34 has no slack. The cov32 attempt proved by exhaustive enumeration that
**34/256 is the exact ceiling of the entire branchless CRAZY/ROTATE family**, and
this rung's threshold is 34. So the rung is not "get a bit further than cov32" —
it is "realise that family's optimum exactly, in the machine." One correct case
short and it fails; there is no configuration with margin.

cov32 shipped the family's 32-point and said so explicitly: the last two hits
"sit on one trit position, not on a new mechanism." That is true as a statement
about the function family. It turned out to be misleading as a statement about
difficulty, because **the operand needed for those two hits is not expressible in
cov32's manufacturing technique at all.** That gap is the whole content of this
rung, and it is why cov34 is a real step rather than a cosmetic one.

## Step 1 — the argmax, not the max

`family_ceiling.py` reports the maximum but not the argument. `argmax.c`
re-enumerates the N = 2, shift-0 family — every `(c1_i, c2_i)` pair at every trit
position `i = 0..5` (9^6) crossed with every additive constant K — and prints
every configuration reaching 34.

There are exactly **nine**, and they are the same function on every input below
243:

    c1 trits 0..3 = 1,  c2 trits 0..3 = 1      -> identity, out = b there
    c1 trit 4    = 1,  c2 trit 4    = 0        -> g_4 = m0 o m1 = (0, 1, 0)
    trits 5..9 contributing the constant 81
    trit 5 of each operand free (the nine variants differ only here, and
    compensate in K; trit 5 of a byte is nonzero on just 13 inputs)

The reading is clean. Identity at 0..3 and the constant 81 give `out = b + 0x51`,
right on the 32 bytes with `b AND 0x51 == 0` — cov32's program. The map
`(0,1,0)` at trit 4 subtracts 162 exactly when `trit_4(b) = 2`, i.e. on
`b` in 162..242, turning `out = b + 81` into `out = b - 81` there. `b - 0x51`
equals `b XOR 0x51` when `b AND 0x51 == 0x51`. That trades away the seven
`b AND 0x51 == 0` hits in that window and buys nine `b AND 0x51 == 0x51` ones.
Net +2, and the verify transcript shows exactly that: the new hits are
209, 211, 213, 215, 217, 219, 221, 223, 241.

**The +2 is forced and unique.** There is no alternative 34-point to fall back on
if this one proves hard to build.

## Step 2 — why cov32's operand family cannot express it

`c2` needs a **zero** trit at position 4. Every operand cov32 manufactures has
the form `rot^k(crazy(0, x))` for a source byte `x`. But `crazy(0, ·)` sends
every trit to 1 or 2 — the `d`-row entries at `a = 0` are `m0(0) = m1(0) = 1`,
`m2(0) = 2` — and rotation only permutes positions. So:

> Every word of the form `rot^k(crazy(0, byte))` has all ten trits in `{1, 2}`.
> The cov32 operand family contains no word with a zero trit anywhere.

cov32's own obstruction argument was about *flat high trits* and was answered by
a rotation. This is a different and stricter invariant, and rotation does not
touch it. A **second CRAZY layer** is structurally required: a zero trit can only
be produced by `m0(1)`, `m0(2)` or `m1(1)`, all of which need an accumulator that
is already nonzero at that position.

There is a second, sharper constraint that kills the obvious repairs. The high
constant must come out to 81, which is **odd**, and the weights `3^i mod 256` for
`i = 5..9` are `243, 217, 139, 161, 227` — all odd. So the trit values
`v_i = m_{c2_i}(m_{c1_i}(0))` must sum to an odd number, and `v_i = 1` requires
`c1_i = 2` **and** `c2_i = 2`. Every construction where `c2`'s high trits come
from a plain source byte gives `v_i` in `{0, 2}` only, hence an even sum, hence
never 81. So `c2` must carry a trit-2 at a high position too — which a byte
operand, whose trits 5..9 are all zero, cannot supply either. Two independent
reasons the naive extension of cov32 fails.

## Step 3 — searching the reachable word space instead of deriving

Rather than push the hand derivation further, `search.c` settles it by brute
force. The whole word space is only 59,049, so the reachable set is computable
exactly. Modelled as a register machine on the accumulator — every op also writes
A into the cell it touched, which is what makes ROT usable at all:

    A <- crazy(A, x(q))   fresh cell q, seed byte x(q)
    A <- rot(A)           re-point D at the cell A was just written to

BFS from `A = 0` reaches **26,944 of 59,049 words** using the 94 seed bytes
available in the pointer-addressable range 34..127. Of those, 243 have `c1`'s
shape and 243 have `c2`'s shape, and scoring every pair by direct simulation
finds many pairs at 34. The rung is constructible.

## Step 4 — the chain, and the two constraints that bite

`chain.c` finds the cheapest realisation. Two things had to go into the search
that are easy to miss:

- **No accumulator reset.** cov32 built its two operands independently and zeroed
  A in between with a scratch CRAZY. That trick needs A to have all trits
  nonzero, and `c2` has a zero trit by construction — so it is unavailable in one
  direction. It is also unnecessary: build `c1` first, leave it parked in its
  cell, and build `c2` **starting from** `A = c1`. The only requirement is that
  the continuation's first op is a CRAZY on a fresh cell, never a ROT, since a
  ROT would rotate `c1`'s cell and destroy it. `chain.c` enforces that as
  `forceCrazyFirst`.
- **Seed bytes are single-use.** Every CRAZY overwrites the cell it reads, and
  `x(·)` is injective on 34..127, so a seed value names exactly one cell. A chain
  that uses the same seed twice is reading a computed value the second time, not
  the seed. The first search returned a 12-op chain that reused seed 53; adding a
  forbidden-seed mask to the second leg's BFS returned a different 12-op chain
  with no reuse and the same score.

The winner, 12 ops:

    CRZ 34 (seed 122), CRZ 35 (seed 53), CRZ 91 (seed 83), ROT 91 x3
                                              -> mem[91] = c1 = 5467
    CRZ 44 (seed 107), CRZ 68 (seed 57), ROT 68 x4
                                              -> mem[68] = c2 = 43780
    IN, CRZ 91, CRZ 68, OUT, HALT             -> crazy(crazy(b, c1), c2) mod 256

    c1 = 5467  trits 0..9 = 1 1 1 1 1 1 1 2 0 0
    c2 = 43780 trits 0..9 = 1 1 1 1 0 0 0 2 0 2

`c2`'s trit 4 is the zero cov32's operand family could not make. The constant
checks out at trit 7, the one high position where **both** operands carry a 2:
`v_7 = m2(m2(0)) = 1`, the single odd term. Trit 9 contributes `v_9 = 2`, all
other high positions contribute 0, and `1*139 + 2*227 = 593 = 81 (mod 256)`.

Layout is cov32's, reused unchanged: every prefix cell holds the unique byte that
decodes to NOP at its own address, so after C walks the prefix, cell `a` holds
`x(a) = encipher(nop_byte(a))`, and a cell with `x(a) = q-1` is a ready-made
"MOVD to q" pointer. That table is genuinely reusable and saved the whole
layout problem here — cov32 was right to call it out.

## What this leaves for the next attempt

- **cov36 is a genuine wall, not a budget problem, for this entire approach.**
  34 is exhaustive over the branchless family; this program attains it. Nothing
  about a longer straight line, a better operand, or more search time moves it.
  cov36 requires input-dependent branching — which is the finite-map/lane problem
  that has two recorded convergent negative results on map12-hi.
- The ranking implication: cov34 sits correctly *above* cov32 and below cov36,
  but the gap cov34→cov36 is a cliff of a different kind than cov32→cov34. This
  rung was a construction problem with a known target; cov36 is an architecture
  problem with no known target. Ranks 18–21 (cov36/40/48/64) are all on the far
  side of that same single wall and are probably not meaningfully ordered
  relative to each other by difficulty — they are one problem, not four.
- Reusable from here: the **register-machine model** in `search.c` (every op
  writes A into its cell, so ROT is `A <- rot(A)` if you re-point D) turns
  "what words can I get into a cell" into a 59,049-node BFS that runs in
  milliseconds. Any future construction on this VM should start by running that
  BFS rather than deriving operand families by hand — it found in seconds what
  two rounds of hand derivation had been circling.
- Also reusable: **chaining operands instead of resetting the accumulator.**
  cov32's zeroing trick is not general (it needs all-nonzero trits); building
  operand N+1 from operand N's value costs nothing and has no precondition
  beyond "don't ROT first."
