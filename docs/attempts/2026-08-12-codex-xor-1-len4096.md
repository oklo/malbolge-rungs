## What changed

The inherited hero1 and hero3 candidates both covered 249/256, but on different
prologues and with different misses:

| candidate | native score | failures |
|---|---:|---|
| hero1 | 249/256 | `0 1 3 8 9 151 255` |
| hero3 | 249/256 | `0 1 3 4 8 9 255` |

The first useful result was an exhaustive scan of every program length from
2305 through 4096 and every legal pair of final opcodes. Prior work correctly
observed that changing the length usually rewrites the crazy-filled tail, but
incorrectly generalized that it must cost the tail-reading successes. There
are phase-aligned exceptions. For both prologue lines, lengths including 2323
and 2605 preserve every one of the original 249 successes.

That aligned extension makes byte 255 routable without paying a tail penalty.
On hero3, a sequence of exact private-block repairs and an extension route
produced an independently verified 250/256 tape, failing
`0 1 3 4 8 9`. More importantly, hero1 already solves byte 4, so it offered a
path beyond that plateau.

The hero1 path was:

1. Extend to 2605 while retaining 249/256.
2. Route byte 255 into writable extension cells. This changes 14 cells and
   trades the byte-255 failure for byte 254.
3. Freeze byte 255 and solve byte 254 with a three-cell repair. The result is
   250/256, failing `0 1 3 8 9 151`.
4. Solve the remaining high failure as a compatibility problem. Byte 151 starts
   at address 1360, which is also executed by solved byte 150. Independent
   witnesses necessarily damage the neighbor. Exhaustively crossing the finite
   12-cell witness families, with address 1360 acting as a JMP, found a joint
   solution for both. This adds byte 151 without losing any other success and
   reaches 251/256.

The final step was found as byte-150 witness 1 crossed with byte-151 witness 66
under opcode order zero. The final tape differs from the 250 tape in 13 cells.

## Negative results and limits of the claims

The remaining failures are all in the shared low window. Inputs 0, 1, and 3
start in the fixed 33-byte prologue. Inputs 8 and 9 execute cells reused as the
operand and output machinery of many solved inputs.

For byte 9, freezing every solved trace leaves one mutable cell and no witness.
Without protection, exact local witnesses exist, but across all eight opcode
branch-order rotations their best raw score was only 237/256. A wider 48-cell
search did worse. This establishes that a direct local patch is not the next
move; it does not prove byte 9 incompatible with all 251 current successes.

Several other searches were bounded and should not be promoted to proofs:

- Exact first-4096-witness compatibility tests found no pair among most low
  inputs. One capped 8/9 crossing existed in a 64-cell model but scored only
  30/256; widening the model to 96 cells found none in the sampled prefixes.
- Single-cell and bounded two-cell shared-window sweeps did not exceed the
  current score.
- Four diversified ten-minute coordinate/descending runs on the earlier hero3
  250 tape stayed at 250.
- Directly splicing hero1's byte-4 cells into the swapped-prologue 250 tape did
  not solve byte 4. An exact nine-cell byte-4 witness sweep found 1815
  witnesses, but their best immediate score was 237/256; transferring that win
  will also require a multi-input compatibility reconstruction.

## Corrections to inherited conclusions

Two inherited structural observations remain sound: stride 9 is forced under
the 4096-byte cap, and low failures cannot simply jump into the extra writable
tape. One conclusion does not: extending beyond 2305 does **not** inevitably
destroy the tail-reading solutions. The memory-fill recurrence has aligned
length phases, and the complete length/pair scan finds them directly. This is
what unlocks byte 255.

The old-prologue line has a hard ceiling below 256 because byte 0 begins on its
fixed `IN`. It is nevertheless the current coverage leader because it solves
byte 4 and the high byte-151 conflict is now removed. A full solution still
requires a prologue or shared-window redesign. A naive transfer of the old
line's byte-4 block to the swapped prologue was tested and failed, so combining
the swapped prologue's reachable byte 0 with the old line's byte-4 and
joint-150/151 structure is itself a coupled reconstruction problem.

## Reproducibility notes

The exact build and search commands, intermediate candidates, and tool index
are in the research [README](../../research/xor-1-len4096-codex/README.md).
Search logs distinguish exhaustive scans from node- or witness-capped evidence.

The structured attempt record deliberately leaves `best_candidate` null. The
current attempt schema validates only `correct_cases/total_cases` within a
single epoch; this rung has one case per epoch and uses 256 epochs to enumerate
the byte domain. Encoding 251/256 in that field would therefore be false even
though the exhaustive native result above is exact. The candidate is still
listed as an artifact and is re-verifiable with the command above.
